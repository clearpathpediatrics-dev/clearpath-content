How to Check If AI Search Mentions Your Business

November 12, 2026 · 7 min read

answer engine optimizationAI search visibilityChatGPT for businessPerplexity SEOlocal AI search

More people are asking ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity for recommendations the way they used to ask Google. "Who's a good commercial HVAC company in Phoenix?" "Best estate planning attorney near me?" If those tools don't mention you, you'll never know unless you check. The good news is that a decent visibility audit takes one afternoon, a spreadsheet, and no special tools.

This is not about chasing a perfect score. It's about finding out, in plain terms, whether the assistants name you, name your competitors, or dodge the question entirely. Once you know that, you can decide what to do about it.

Build your prompt list first

The mistake most people make is typing one vague question, getting a weird answer, and declaring the whole exercise broken. You need a structured list of prompts that mirror how real buyers ask. Spend 20 minutes writing these before you touch any assistant.

Cover four prompt types:

  • Direct recommendation: "Who are the best [service] companies in [city]?" This is the money question. It's where recommendations actually happen.
  • Named lookup: "What do you know about [Your Business Name]?" This shows whether the assistant has any profile of you at all, and whether the facts are right.
  • Problem-first: "My [specific problem], who should I call in [area]?" For example, "My commercial walk-in freezer keeps icing up, who repairs that in Tucson?" Buyers describe symptoms, not service categories.
  • Comparison: "Is [Competitor] or [You] better for [specific need]?" Tells you how you're framed against rivals.

Aim for 10 to 15 prompts total. Write them in a spreadsheet with columns for the prompt, then one column each for ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity. You'll paste answers or notes into those cells.

Run the same prompts across all three tools

Open all three assistants in separate browser tabs. Run each prompt in each tool and record what comes back. You're looking for a few specific things, not the whole wall of text.

For each answer, note:

  1. Are you mentioned? Yes, no, or mentioned-but-buried.
  2. Who else is mentioned? List the competitors named. This is your real-world competitor set for AI search, which may differ from who you think you compete with.
  3. Is the information accurate? Wrong phone number, outdated service area, services you dropped years ago, a location you closed.
  4. Where did it pull from? Perplexity shows citations directly. ChatGPT and Gemini sometimes cite sources when browsing. Note the domains that feed the answer.

A few practical notes on each tool

Perplexity is the most useful for an audit because it cites sources inline, so you can see exactly which pages shaped the answer. Click through the citations and you'll often find a directory, a "best of" listicle, or a competitor's page doing the heavy lifting.

ChatGPT behaves differently depending on whether it's using its built-in knowledge or browsing the live web. If you have web search enabled, ask it to search. If not, it may answer from training data, which lags behind. Test both modes if you can, and label which one you used.

Gemini is tied closely to Google's index, so its answers often track what ranks in regular Google search. If you're invisible in Gemini, check your standard search results for the same query.

How do I know if ChatGPT recommends my business?

Ask ChatGPT directly, "Who are the best [your service] providers in [your city]?" and look at whether your name appears, where it sits in the list, and whether the description matches reality. If you're not named, try the problem-first version of the question, because category queries and symptom queries can return completely different businesses. Run the same prompt two or three times in fresh chats, since answers vary between sessions. If you never appear across several phrasings and sessions, ChatGPT doesn't currently have a strong enough signal to recommend you, and the fix is usually getting mentioned on the third-party pages and directories the assistant draws from, not editing your own homepage.

Score what you find

After you've run everything, you'll have a full grid. Turn it into a simple score so you can track progress later. A quick scheme that works:

ResultPoints
Named first or in top 33
Named lower in the list2
Mentioned only when asked by name1
Not mentioned, or facts wrong0

Score each prompt per tool and total it. The number itself doesn't matter. What matters is running the same prompts again in 60 or 90 days and seeing whether it moved.

Worked example

Suppose a dental practice in Columbus runs the prompt "best cosmetic dentist in Columbus Ohio" across all three tools. ChatGPT names four practices, none of them theirs. Gemini names three, theirs is third. Perplexity names five and cites two local "top dentists" listicles plus a directory. The practice isn't on either listicle.

That's a clear read. They have some visibility (Gemini knows them, likely from Google), but the sources shaping AI answers — the two listicles and the directory — don't include them. The action item writes itself: get reviewed by whoever runs those listicles, claim and complete the directory profile, and publish content that those sources tend to cite. No guesswork required.

What should I do if AI tools get my business facts wrong?

Fix the facts at the source, because assistants don't invent details, they pull them from pages they trust. Start with the properties you control: your Google Business Profile, your website's contact and services pages, and major directories like Yelp, industry-specific listings, and your chamber of commerce page. Make sure your name, address, phone, hours, and service list are identical everywhere, since conflicting information makes assistants either pick the wrong version or skip you. Then look at the third-party pages the assistants actually cited in your audit and request corrections where you can. Changes don't appear instantly; the assistants refresh their picture of you on their own schedules, so recheck in a month or two rather than the next day.

Turn the audit into an action list

By the end of the afternoon, your grid should point to three or four concrete moves. The patterns are usually obvious once the data is in front of you:

  • You're invisible everywhere, competitors aren't: The pages feeding these answers don't mention you at all. You need presence on the directories and roundup pages that keep showing up in citations, plus your own content that answers the buyer questions directly.
  • You show up in Gemini but not ChatGPT or Perplexity: You likely rank in regular Google but lack the broader web footprint the other tools lean on. Focus on getting cited beyond your own site.
  • You're mentioned but described wrong: This is a data-consistency problem, not a visibility problem. Clean up your profiles and the source pages.
  • You appear only when named directly: The assistants know you exist but don't consider you a strong answer to the open-ended "who's best" questions. That gap closes when more credible pages associate you with the specific service and location.

The practical takeaway

You can't improve what you've never measured. Spend one afternoon building a 12-prompt list, running it across ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity, and recording whether you're named, who beats you, and which sources feed the answers. Save the grid. The sources you spot in the citations are your real to-do list, and re-running the same prompts in 90 days is the only honest way to tell whether anything you did made a difference.
