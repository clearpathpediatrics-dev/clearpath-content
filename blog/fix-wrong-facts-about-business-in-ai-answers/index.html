How to Fix Wrong Facts About Your Business in AI Answers

November 16, 2026 · 7 min read

AI searchanswer engine optimizationbusiness reputationstructured datalocal SEO

Someone asks an AI assistant whether your firm handles commercial cases. It says no. You do. Or it quotes a starting price you abandoned two years ago, or lists a phone number that rings a disconnected line. These errors do real damage because the person asking never sees your website — they get a confident summary and move on.

Correcting a wrong fact inside an AI answer is not the same as editing your own site. The model is pulling from somewhere, and often from several somewheres that disagree. The job is to find what it is reading, fix the underlying sources, and make the correct version easier to trust than the wrong one. Here is how that works in practice.

Why AI assistants state wrong facts about companies

AI assistants state wrong facts because they summarize from sources that are outdated, contradictory, or scraped from a third party you do not control. The model is not making things up out of nowhere most of the time — it is repeating something it found and weighting it as reliable.

The common causes break down like this:

  • Stale pages that still rank. An old service page, a press release from 2019, or a cached version of your pricing. If it is still indexed, it is still a source.
  • Third-party directories. Yelp, Yellow Pages, industry listings, and aggregators often carry old hours, a former address, or a service list you never updated. These get scraped constantly.
  • Your own contradictions. Your homepage says you serve three counties, your footer says one city, and a blog post mentions a fourth county you tested once. The model has no way to know which is current.
  • A confident competitor or review. A review that says "they don't do emergency calls" can become the model's fact, especially if your own site never clearly states that you do.
  • Name confusion. Another business with a similar name, and the model merges the two.

The point is that you rarely fix an AI answer by arguing with the AI. You fix the sources it trusts.

Find the exact error before you touch anything

Before you can correct anything, you need the precise wrong claim and, where possible, where it came from. Vague complaints — "the AI says wrong stuff about us" — do not lead to a fix. A specific, quotable error does.

Run this sequence:

  1. Ask the question the way a customer would. Use ChatGPT, Google's AI overviews, Perplexity, and Gemini. Phrase it plainly: "Does [Company] handle [service]?" and "What are [Company]'s hours?" Write down the exact wrong wording.
  2. Ask for sources. On tools that cite (Perplexity, Google's AI overviews, often Bing), ask "Where did you get that?" or click the citations. This frequently points you straight at the stale page or directory.
  3. Search the wrong fact itself. If the AI says your rate starts at $89 and it is actually $129, search "$89" plus your business name. You will usually find the page carrying the old number.
  4. Check the obvious directories by hand. Google Business Profile, Yelp, Bing Places, Apple Maps, and the two or three biggest directories in your industry. Note every one that disagrees with reality.
  5. Audit your own site for contradictions. Search site:yourdomain.com for the topic. Count how many pages state a fact and whether they agree.

By the end you should have a short list: the wrong claim, every place it appears, and which of those you can edit directly.

Fix the sources in priority order

Not all sources carry equal weight. Fix the ones you control first, because they are fast and they are what the model treats as the primary account of your own business.

1. Your own website, made unambiguous

State the correct fact plainly, in text, on the most relevant page. Not in an image, not in a PDF, not buried in a paragraph. If your service area is three counties, write a sentence: "We serve Pima, Pinal, and Santa Cruz counties." Then kill the contradictions — update or redirect the old pages that say something different. One clear, repeated fact across your site beats one correct page fighting four stale ones.

2. Structured data

Add or correct schema markup for the facts that get mangled most: LocalBusiness for name, address, phone, hours, and area served; Service or Offer for what you do and price ranges; FAQPage for direct question-answer pairs. Structured data does not force a model to agree, but it gives a machine-readable version of the truth that is hard to misread.

3. Google Business Profile and major directories

Correct your GBP first — it feeds a lot of answers. Then work through the directories from your audit. Many let you claim the listing and edit directly. For stubborn aggregators, use their correction form. This is tedious and worth it.

4. The third-party page you cannot edit

If a wrong fact lives on a site you do not control — a journalist's old article, a partner's outdated page — email them with the correction and the current fact. Many will update. If they will not, your remaining move is to make the correct version so much stronger and more current across everything else that it outweighs the one holdout.

A worked example

Suppose a dental practice in Columbus finds that ChatGPT says they do not accept new patients and that they are closed on Fridays. Both are wrong — they accept new patients and they are open Friday 8 to 1.

  • Trace: Perplexity cites an old "we're not accepting new patients" banner from a 2021 blog post that is still indexed, plus a Yelp listing showing Friday as closed.
  • Fix the site: Delete the 2021 banner text, publish a current page that says "We are accepting new patients as of [month, year]" in plain text, and add a visible hours block listing Friday 8 AM–1 PM.
  • Schema: Update LocalBusiness opening hours to include Friday, and add an FAQPage entry: "Are you accepting new patients?" with a one-line yes.
  • Directories: Correct Friday hours in GBP, Yelp, and Apple Maps.
  • Recheck in a few weeks: Models update on their own cadence, not on demand. Re-ask the questions and confirm the answer has shifted.

The error came from two sources, so fixing one would have left the model half-wrong. That is the usual pattern.

How long does it take for an AI answer to update after you fix the source?

It varies from days to several months, and there is no setting that forces it. Answer engines refresh at different rates — some pull near-live search results and update within days once your corrected page is indexed, while others rely on a training snapshot that only changes when the model is updated. The practical implication is that you fix everything you can, confirm the corrected pages are indexed, and then re-check on a schedule rather than waiting for a notification. Do not assume a single fix failed because the answer is unchanged a week later.

Can you ask an AI company to correct a fact directly?

Some platforms offer feedback mechanisms, but they are not a reliable primary fix. You can thumbs-down an answer or use a reporting link, and for factual errors about a real business that is worth doing — it creates a record. But these channels are slow, opaque, and rarely confirm action. Treat them as a supplement to source correction, never a substitute. The practical takeaway

Wrong facts in AI answers are almost always downstream of wrong or conflicting sources. Find the exact claim, trace where it is coming from, fix your own site and structured data first, then clean up directories and third parties. Make the correct fact consistent everywhere and stated in plain text. Then recheck on a schedule, because the update happens on the model's clock, not yours. Treat it as source hygiene, not a one-time edit, and the errors stop recurring.
