Google Business Profile Fixes That Get You Calls

August 23, 2026 · 8 min read

google business profilelocal seomap pack rankinggbp optimisationlocal search visibility

Your Google Business Profile is the single biggest lever you have in local search, and most of it is filled out wrong or left half-done. The problem is that the advice online treats every field as equally important. It is not. Some fields directly influence whether you show up in the three-pack on a phone. Others are cosmetic. If you spend your hour a week posting daily updates while your primary category is wrong, you are polishing the wrong thing.

This is a ranked walkthrough of what actually moves the needle, based on how Google's local algorithm weighs relevance, distance, and prominence. No filler fields.

Get your primary category right before anything else

The primary category is the strongest single relevance signal on your profile, and choosing the wrong one caps how far you can rank no matter what else you do. Google uses it to decide which searches you are even eligible for. A business listed as "Contractor" when it should be "HVAC Contractor" is competing in a broader, less relevant pool and losing to firms with the exact match.

Pick the most specific category that describes your core money-making service. Then add secondary categories for the other services you actually perform. Do not stuff them. Ten categories that dilute your focus can hurt more than three tight ones.

How to find the right categories

  • Search the terms your customers use, look at the businesses ranking in the map pack, and check their primary category. You can see it in the page source or with a free GBP audit tool.
  • Match the category to the service, not to a keyword you want to rank for. Google validates categories against your actual business.
  • Suppose a firm does both plumbing and drain cleaning. Primary: Plumber. Secondary: Drainage service. Not "Emergency plumber," which is not a real category and signals you are guessing.

What actually affects Google Business Profile ranking?

The three factors Google weighs are relevance, distance, and prominence, and you can only directly control two of them. Relevance is how well your profile matches the search, driven mostly by your categories, business name, and the services and description you fill in. Distance is proximity between the searcher and your location, which you cannot change beyond having a real address in your service area. Prominence is how well-known and trusted you appear, driven by review volume and quality, your presence across the web, and links to your site.

Everything worth doing on your profile ladders up to relevance or prominence. Daily posts, holiday hours, and product tiles are fine, but they are not ranking factors in any meaningful sense. Spend your effort where the algorithm is actually looking: categories, reviews, a consistent name and address across the web, and the services you list.

Reviews: volume, recency, and the words in them

Reviews influence both prominence and relevance, and the text inside them does more work than most people realise. When a customer writes "they fixed our furnace the same day," that phrase becomes associated with your profile. A steady flow of reviews mentioning your actual services and city names is worth more than a big pile of five-star reviews with no text.

Recency matters too. A profile with 40 reviews where the last one was eight months ago looks stale next to a competitor pulling in two a week. You want a repeatable system, not a one-time push.

A review system that runs itself

  1. Create a short review link from your profile (the "Ask for reviews" button generates one) and shorten it so it is easy to text.
  2. Send the request within 24 hours of finishing the job, by text, while the experience is fresh. Text open rates crush email for this.
  3. Have the technician or account manager mention it in person first: "I'm going to send you a quick link, it takes 30 seconds and really helps us." That personal ask roughly doubles follow-through in most operations.
  4. Respond to every review, positive and negative, within a couple of days. Work the service and city naturally into your replies without sounding robotic.
  5. Never buy reviews or offer discounts for them. Google filters fakes and can suspend profiles for incentivised reviews.

The description, services, and attributes that pull weight

The services section is underused and it feeds relevance directly. List each service you offer as its own entry with a real description. If you are a dental practice, that means separate entries for cleanings, crowns, implants, emergency visits, and whatever else you bill for, each with a sentence or two in plain language.

The business description (750 characters) is lower impact but still worth doing well. Front-load your main service and city in the first sentence, because that is what gets shown and read. Skip the marketing adjectives. "Family-owned HVAC repair and installation serving Tucson since 2004" beats "Your trusted partner for all your comfort needs" every time.

Attributes (wheelchair accessible, women-owned, free estimates, appointment required) are minor for ranking but they show up as filters and badges that affect click-through. Fill in the ones that are true.

Photos and posts: useful, but keep them in proportion

Photos do not rank you, but they affect whether someone who sees you actually calls. Profiles with real, current photos of the team, trucks, storefront, and finished work get more engagement than stock imagery or an empty gallery. Add a handful of genuine photos every month. Geotagging and file naming have negligible effect despite what you will read elsewhere.

Posts (the update feature) keep the profile looking active and can surface offers, but treat them as a nice-to-have. Posting once a week with something real to say is plenty. Posting daily to hit an arbitrary quota is time you could spend collecting reviews.

The one-hour optimisation checklist

If you have never done a proper pass, block an hour and work through this in order:

  • Primary category — set to the most specific accurate option. Add relevant secondary categories only.
  • Business name — exactly as it appears on your signage and legal registration. No keyword stuffing; that risks suspension.
  • Address and service area — accurate and matching your website and other listings letter for letter.
  • Phone number — a local number that matches your site, not a call-tracking number that differs from what is listed elsewhere.
  • Hours — correct, including holiday hours, so you do not show as open when you are closed.
  • Services — every billable service listed separately with a short real description.
  • Description — main service and city in the first sentence.
  • Photos — at least 10 real, current images.
  • Review link — created, shortened, and saved somewhere your team can grab it.
  • Website link — pointing to the most relevant page, ideally a location or service page, not just the homepage.

How long before Google Business Profile changes show up?

Category and information changes can register within days, but ranking movement from reviews and prominence builds over weeks and months, and results genuinely vary by market. A category correction in a low-competition town might show up in the map pack within a week or two. Building enough review momentum and web presence to outrank an established competitor in a dense city can take several months of consistent effort. Anyone promising a specific position by a specific date is guessing.

The compounding part is reviews and the pages you link to. A profile fed by a steady review stream and supported by service and location pages on your own site keeps climbing while a set-it-and-forget-it profile stalls. That link between your profile and genuinely useful pages on your site is where the two halves of local visibility meet, and it is the part most businesses neglect. Building out that supporting content is exactly the kind of program firms like ClearPath Content run when the profile alone has hit its ceiling.

The takeaway: fix your primary category first, build a review request into your job-completion routine, and list every service as its own entry. Those three things account for most of the ranking difference between the profiles that show up and the ones that do not. Do them properly once, keep the reviews flowing, and leave the daily-posting busywork to your competitors.
